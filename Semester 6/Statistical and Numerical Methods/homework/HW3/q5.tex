\section{Measurements with multiple categories}
\subsection{Estimators}
First the linear fit is 
\begin{equation}
    x=x_0+vt
\end{equation}
using the formulas from training 6 
\begin{align}
    \hat{v} = \frac{\overline{tx}-\overline{t}\overline{x}}{\overline{t^2}-\overline{t}^2} && \hat{x_0} = \overline{x}-\hat{v}\overline{t}
\end{align}
and
\begin{align}
    \sigma_{\hat{v}}^2=\frac{\sigma^2}{N\ins{\overline{t^2}-\overline{t}^2}} && \sigma_{\hat{x_0}}^2 = \frac{\sigma^2 \overline{t^2}}{N\ins{\overline{t^2}-\overline{t}^2}}
\end{align}
calculating using python
\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{c|cc}
        Cart & velocity estimate (cm/s) & estimate error\\
        \hline
        1 & 0.280 & 0.095\\
        2 & 0.280 & 0.095\\
        3 & 0.280 & 0.095\\
        4 & 0.253 & 0.095
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}
\subsection{chi square}
by definition
\begin{equation}
    \chi^2 = \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ins{x_i - x_0 - vt_i}^2
\end{equation}
plugging the numbers in using python 
\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{c|cc}
        Cart & chi-square\\
        \hline
        1 & 13.75 \\
        2 & 13.75 \\
        3 & 13.75 \\
        4 & 14.77
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}
the chi-square is higher than the degrees of freedom (9, from 11 measurements minus 2 parameters) for all the carts, so none of the measurements appear to be indicative of a constant velocity.
