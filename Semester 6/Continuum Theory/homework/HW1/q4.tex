\section{Bouyance force - hot air balloon}
\begin{colbox}
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item Write the atmospheric density profile \(\rho(z)\) as a function of the height z.
		\item We want to treat the hot air balloon as a point particle; to do so, we want to ensure that density changes are small relative to the size of the hot air balloon. This can be required by the condition \(\frac{R}{\rho(z)}\pdv{\rho}{z}\ll 1\). What is the resulting condition on the balloon's radius \(R\)? Is it satisfied for the problem's parameters?
		\item Treat the ballon as a point particle at height \(z\) (meaning the air density around it is \(\rho(z)\)) and write the net force acting on the balloon together iwth the basket. What is the condition for the balloon to remain at a certain height? Find the height \(z\) to which the hot air balloon reaches.
	\end{enumerate}
\end{colbox}
\subsection{Density Profile}
Since we assume the atmosphere is an ideal gas,
\begin{equation}
	pV = N k_B T \implies p = \frac{N}{V}k_B T
\end{equation}
but
\begin{equation}
	\frac{N}{V} = n = \frac{\rho}{m}
\end{equation}
therefore
\begin{equation}
	p = \frac{\rho k_B T}{m}
\end{equation}
inserting into the hydrostatic equation
\begin{equation}
	\grad{p} = \rho\vb{f}
\end{equation}
we get
\begin{equation}
	\frac{k_B T}{m}\dv{\rho}{z} = -\rho g
\end{equation}
which is an ODE with the solution
\begin{equation}
	\rho(z) = \rho(0) e^{-\frac{z}{l_a}}
\end{equation}
\subsection{Point Balloon}
We require that
\begin{equation}
	\frac{R}{\rho(z)}\pdv{\rho}{z}\ll 1 \implies \dv{\rho}{z} \ll \frac{\rho(z)}{R}
\end{equation}
if we take the derivative of the density profile we found
\begin{equation}
	\dv{\rho}{z} = -\frac{\rho(0)}{l_a}e^{-\frac{z}{l_a}}
\end{equation}
and inserting the profile we find 
\begin{equation}
	-\frac{\rho(0)}{l_a}e^{-\frac{z}{l_a}} \ll \frac{\rho(0) e^{-\frac{z}{l_a}}}{R}
\end{equation}
simplifying
\begin{equation}
	-\frac{1}{l_a} \ll \frac{1}{R}
\end{equation}
therefore
\begin{equation}
	l_a \gg R
\end{equation}
in our question \(R\) is 3 orders of magnitude smaller than \(l_a\) therefore the condition is satisfied.
\subsection{Constant Height Condition}
The balloon is affected by the following forces: its own gravitational pull, the basket's gravitational pull, and the bouyancy force from the balloon. The balloon will reach the height at which it is at equilibrium:
\begin{equation}
	\rho_{hot} V g = \rho(z) V g + Mg
\end{equation}
rearranging
\begin{equation}
	\rho(z) = \frac{\rho_{hot} V g + Mg}{Vg} = \frac{\rho_{hot}V + M}{V}
\end{equation}
Inserting the expression for the density
\begin{equation}
	 e^{-\frac{z}{l_a}} = \frac{\rho_{hot}V + M}{\rho(0) V}
\end{equation}
therefore
\begin{equation}
	z = -l_a \ln{\frac{\rho_{hot}V + M}{\rho(0) V}}
\end{equation}
inserting the numbers we find
\begin{equation}
	z = \cdots = 1.595 \km
\end{equation}
