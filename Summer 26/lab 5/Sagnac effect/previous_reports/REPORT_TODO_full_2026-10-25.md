## The story (read this first)

The report tests one prediction. Two beams sent around the same fiber loop in opposite directions come back with a phase difference that grows with the rotation rate, Δφ = a_th ω, and the coefficient a_th = 8π²NR²/(λc) is fixed by the loop's turns and radius and by the wavelength (Section 1). Testing it needs two measured quantities. ω: turns timed at nine controller settings give the table's angular velocity, and a straight line through them converts the settings of the photographs, each to 1.3 % (2.1). Δφ: the fringes are rings evenly spaced in r², rotation slides them without changing their spacing, and the phase at the ring center follows from the squared radii of the dark rings, the manual's Eq. 7 carried out in r²; over the run the pattern moves by half a fringe (2.2). The test: Δφ is linear in ω with slope 1.83 ± 0.06 rad/(rad s⁻¹) once the scatter of the settings, the choice of phase readout and the calibration are all counted, against the predicted 1.7 ± 0.1; they agree at 0.6σ, which read the other way is c = (2.9 ± 0.2) × 10⁸ m/s (2.3). What limits the test: the given turn count sets the prediction's 8 %; our 3.5 % comes mostly from how the phase is read; the phase wanders slowly, and a steady drift cannot be separated from the slope because the speeds were taken in increasing order; the table's speed bends away from the line at the top; the sign of the shift was not recorded (2.4).

Each section hands the next one quantity: 2.1 hands ω, 2.2 hands Δφ, 2.3 hands the slope and its comparison, 2.4 says what to change next time. Nothing is derived twice: the slope's three uncertainty parts are built in 2.3, with their kinds; 2.4 does not repeat them, it says what they leave out.

## Section plans

### 1.1 Theory

**1.1.1 The Sagnac phase.** Purpose: the formula tested, derived (prep Q4); the subsection ends with the coefficient the experiment measures.
- Lab frame, loop of radius R turning at ω, the splitter moves at v = ωR, light at c both ways: t± = l/(c ∓ v), l = 2πR, so Δt = 2lv/(c² − v²) ≈ 4Aω/c² with A = πR² (manual Eq. 1–2; Pascoli Eq. 1; Tartaglia Eq. 1). N turns: Δt = 4NAω/c².
- A delay is a phase 2π(c/λ)Δt: **Δφ = 8πNAω/(λc)** (manual Eq. 3–5; Schwartz Eq. 1). Label `eq:sagnac`.
- Independent of where the axis is (Schwartz Eq. 4, prep Q8) and of the fiber's refractive index (Schwartz Sec. 2, prep Q10): one sentence each, cited. These two explain why only N, R, λ enter.
- Optional: first order in v/c; at the rim v ≈ 1 m/s, v/c ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁹, and relativity changes Δt only at order (v/c)² (Pascoli Sec. 3; Tartaglia). The effect is measurable because interference resolves a fraction of an optical period (2.13 fs).
- The sign of Δφ follows the sense of rotation (needed for 2.4: not checked).
- Close with what the experiment measures: Δφ against ω should be a straight line through the origin with slope **a_th = 8π²NR²/(λc)**; everything the test needs from theory is this coefficient.

**1.1.2 Interference and the ring pattern.** Purpose: why the fringes are rings, evenly spaced in r², and how a photograph becomes a phase.
- Two coherent beams: I = I₁ + I₂ + 2√(I₁I₂) cos φ (manual Eq. 6, p. 5); dark where φ = π + 2πm. Plane waves at an angle give straight fringes with one period λ/(2 sin θ) (manual Eq. 6 and Fig. 2; prep Q1–Q2); ours are rings, as Schwartz's are (Sec. 3, p. 6: his pattern is not linear either).
- A Gaussian beam carries the transverse phase kρ²/(2R(z)) (QE3, PDF p. 10; prep Q3). Two beams on a common axis with wavefront radii R₁ ≠ R₂ at the camera differ by **φ(r) = 2πr²/P + φ_c, P = 2λ/|1/R₁ − 1/R₂|**. Label `eq:rings`. Equal spacing in r² (like Newton's rings).
- Dark rings at **R_m² = P(m + η), φ_c = π − 2πη**. Label `eq:dark_rings`. Rotation adds the same phase everywhere, so it changes only φ_c: the rings slide in or out. A tilt moves the center; a lens–fiber distance changes P (prep Q7); neither moves φ_c (to first order).
- The readout in words: locate the dark rings, fit the line of R_m² against m; its slope is P and its intercept the phase. This is the manual's Eq. 7 (shift of each minimum over the local fringe period) and Schwartz's Eq. 5 (Δφ = 2πΔx/δ), done in r², where every ring has the same period P. η is a ratio of two lengths squared, so no pixel-to-length calibration is needed.
- **Attribution, for a reader who knows only the lab documents** (the r² readout is not in them as a rule; this chain shows it is their method, not ours):
  1. Manual p. 5: Eq. 7 reads Δφ as the average over the minima of the shift of each minimum over its period, Δr_i/Λ_i, and writes the period per minimum, Λ_i: the manual itself lets the period differ from minimum to minimum. Its picture (Eq. 6, Fig. 2) is a pattern with one period Λ.
  2. Schwartz p. 6: circular fringes; he says the phase per fringe depends on the fringe width, which differs between inner and outer fringes, and reads Δφ = 2πΔx/δ with an estimated width (Eq. 5; average width 61 px, p. 7). That is Eq. 7 with a locally measured period.
  3. Why that is not enough here: a reading over a width is exact only while the width is the same over the shift. Ours reach half a fringe and the width changes under the moving ring: the first dark ring moves 157 px between rest and the top speed, while its gap to the next ring is 159 px at rest and 242 px at the top speed, so Eq. 7 in r gives 6.2, 4.1 or 4.9 rad depending on the width taken, for a shift of 3.30 rad (RESULTS Task 2).
  4. The variable in which the period does not change: the Gaussian beam of prep Q3 (QE3, PDF p. 10; our entry report, Eq. 11, writes its phase k r²/(2R(z))) carries a transverse phase proportional to ρ², so two beams of different curvature differ by a phase proportional to r² (`eq:rings`): in r² every ring, in every photo, has the one period P. Eq. 7 in r² is exact, and it is still Eq. 7, with Λ_i = P for every i.
  5. Checked on the data, not assumed: in the 126 photos with three dark rings the two gaps in r² agree to 2 % of a fringe with no systematic difference, while in r they differ by a factor 1.35–1.86 (RESULTS Task 2); Fig. 5(b) shows the straight line.
  6. What to call it in the report: the manual's Eq. 7 (Schwartz's Eq. 5) carried out in r², where the period is the same for every ring; cite manual p. 5, Schwartz p. 6, QE3 p. 10 and prep Q3 at that sentence. One optional sentence with the numbers of item 3 as the reason. The formula for P in the wavefront radii is optional: the report uses it only to say that refocusing changes P and not the phase (prep Q7). Say in 2.2 how Fig. 5(b) is Eq. 7: R_m²/P is the position of dark ring m in units of the fringe period, so the offset between the rest line and the moving line is the shift of each dark ring over its period, averaged over the rings (Eq. 7's ⟨Δr_i/Λ_i⟩ with r² for r and P for Λ_i), and 2π times it is Δφ; Schwartz's Eq. 5 is the same with his width δ.

### 2.1 Rotation calibration (task 1)

Purpose: the conversion from S to ω that the photos need; task 1 asks for the linear ratio. Fig. 2, Table 1.
- At each setting the mean turn time T gives ω = 2π/T. Its uncertainty has two parts: the spread of the single turns, and the reaction time of the stopwatch presses (0.25 s per press, our own reaction time measured with an online test). The turns are consecutive, so the mean is set by the first and last press only, and the reaction-time part is √2 × 0.25 s divided by the number of turns. Together 0.15–1.1 % of ω, the reaction time dominating at the fast settings. One worked example, S = 90: 10 turns, ⟨T⟩ = 3.545 s, u(T) = 0.038 s, ω = 1.772 ± 0.019 rad/s. Table 1.

The points lie farther from the line than their error bars allow (χ²/dof = 20.2/7 = 2.9, p = 0.005): the table turns slower than linear at the fast settings, 3.0 % below the line at S = 90 (line 1.826, timed 1.772 rad/s). ### 2.2 The fringe pattern and its phase (tasks 2 and 3)

Purpose: what rotation does to the pattern, and how each photograph becomes a phase. Fig. 3, Fig. 5, Table 2.
- Rings; as ω rises they contract: the first dark ring moves from 258 px at rest to 101 px at the top speed, and the center goes from bright to nearly dark, about half a fringe (Fig. 3).
- Evenly spaced in r², not in r (`eq:rings` from 1.1.2; one clause, no figure: Fig. 5(b) shows the straight line): in the fastest photo the dark rings are 241 px and then 132 px apart in r, but 1.08 × 10⁵ px² both times in r²; over the 126 photos with three rings the two gaps in r² agree to 2 % of a fringe. Ring spacing P: median 1.07 × 10⁵ px²; per-setting values within ±1.7 % (Table 2).
- Rotation changes only φ_c (1.1.2): the pattern slides, P stays. The camera never saturates, and the position of a minimum does not depend on the camera's intensity response.
- The phase of one photo (Fig. 5): the dark rings are located and concentric circles fitted to them, said in two sentences (along lines from the ring center the intensity has a minimum at each dark ring; the minima are fitted with concentric circles, one common center and one radius per ring; the located minima scatter about 5 px rms about the circles, against 130–240 px between rings); the line R_m² = P(m + η) through the two or three rings (`eq:dark_rings`) gives φ_c. Call it what it is: the manual's Eq. 7 carried out in r² (1.1.2, attribution). P is fitted in every photo, so a change of the ring spacing does not move φ_c. η stays between 0 and 1 for the whole run (0.62 at rest, 0.10 at the top speed), so the ring numbering never shifts and there is no 2π ambiguity; Fig. 5(b) shows the two intercepts 0.52 fringe apart.
- Δφ = φ_c minus its mean over the 10 rest photos. Setting means (Table 2): mean of the photos, with the uncertainty of a setting mean, 0.04 rad for every setting: the rms scatter of the ten settings about the line of 2.3 (forward reference, accepted 24 October). Say why once: the photographs of a setting are not independent, the phase wanders slowly over minutes (Fig. 8(b), here since 25 October), so the photo-to-photo standard errors (0.004–0.028 rad) are only the photo noise and understate the uncertainty of a mean; the scatter about the line contains the noise, the wander and the calibration's effect on a point.
- One sentence: the same photographs can be read in two other ways (a cosine fitted to the intensity, each profile matched to the rest profile); they are compared in 2.3.
- **Attribution:** the readout itself is traced in 1.1.2. Besides it: (1) Δφ is the shift of the pattern at the n-th angular velocity relative to the pattern at rest, which is what the manual's Δr_i(n) in Eq. 7 and Schwartz's "relative dark fringe shift" (p. 6) mean; our rest reference is the mean of the 10 rest photos. (2) Eq. 7 averages over the minima; the line through the two or three rings is that average, in r². (3) Several photos per setting, averaged with their standard error, is our choice (Schwartz records one pattern per angular frequency, p. 6); one clause. (4) The ring-numbering check, η between 0 and 1, is ours; one clause.
- Outcome: Δφ for the ten settings, 0 to 3.29 rad, half a fringe at the top speed (Table 2).
